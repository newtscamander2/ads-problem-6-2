\begin{exercise}[D'ary heap]
	A d-ary heap is like a binary heap, but (with one possible exception)
	nonleaf nodes have d children instead of two children. In all parts of
	this problem, assume that the time to maintain the mapping between
	objects and heap elements is O(1) per operation.

	a. Describe how to represent a d-ary heap in an array.

	b. Using $\theta$-notation, express the height of a d-ary heap of n elements in
	terms of n and d.

	c. Give an efficient implementation of EXTRACT-MAX in a d-ary
	max-heap. Analyze its running time in terms of d and n.

	d. Give an efficient implementation of INCREASE-KEY in a d-ary
	max-heap. Analyze its running time in terms of d and n.

	e. Give an efficient implementation of INSERT in a d-ary max-heap.
	Analyze its running time in terms of d and n.

\end{exercise}


\section*{Comment}

\section*{Our solution}

\section*{a}
A d'ary array can be represented using its children. For the left child of a given node, the index of the child is $d \cdot i$, and for the right child, the index is one larger than the left child, meaning $d \cdot i + 1$.

$$i \cdot d$$
$$i \cdot d + 1$$

\section*{b}
To find the height of a d'ary heap in terms of $n$ and $d$ where $n$ is the number of elements in the sequence and $d$ is the maximum amount of children, we can use logarithm
$$h = \log_d{n}$$

\section*{c}
\begin{center}
	\begin{minipage}{12cm}
		\begin{algorithmic}
			\Procedure{Max-Heap-Maximum}{$A$}
			\If{$\text{A.heap-size} < 1$}
			\State{$\text{\textbf{error} "heap underflow"}$}

			\EndIf

			\State{\Return{$A[1]$}}
			\EndProcedure

			\\
			\\
			\Procedure{Max-Heapify}{$A, i$}

			\State{$largest = i$}

			\For{$x = 0 \rightarrow d-1$}
			\State{$index = i+i\cdot d + x$}
			\If{$index \leq \text{A.heap-size} \text{ and } A[index] > A[largest]$}
			\State{$largest = index$}
			\EndIf
			\EndFor

			\If{$largest \neq i$}
			\State{$\text{Exchange }A[i] \text{ with } A[largest]$}
			\State{$\text{Max-Heapify}(A, largest)$}
			\EndIf

			\EndProcedure

			\\
			\\
			\Procedure{Extract-Max}{$A$}
			\State{$max = \text{Max-Heap-Maximum}(A)$}
			\State{$A[1] = A[\text{A.heap-size}]$}
			\State{$\text{Max-Heapify}(A, 1)$}
			\State{\Return{$max$}}
			\EndProcedure
		\end{algorithmic}
	\end{minipage}
\end{center}

Extract-Max does a constant amount of work on-top of Max-Heapify.
We therefore have to find the running-time of Max-Heapify to find the running-time of our program.
Max-heapify contains a loop from $0 \rightarrow d-1$ so it runs for $d$ children for all nodes under the highest node.
You can find the height of the heap using $log_d{n}$.
The running-time for our implementation is therefore
$$3 \cdot log_d{(n)} = O(log_d{(n)})$$

\section*{d}
\begin{center}
	\begin{minipage}{12cm}
		\begin{algorithmic}
			\Procedure{MAX-HEAP-INCREASE-KEY}{$A, x, k$}
			\If{$k < x.key$}
			\State{$\text{\textbf{error} "new key is smaller than current key"}$}
			\State{$x.key = k$}

			\EndIf{}

			\EndProcedure{}
		\end{algorithmic}
	\end{minipage}
\end{center}

\begin{center}

		\begin{algorithmic}
			\Procedure{Max-Heap-Insert}{$A, x, k$}
			\If{$A.heap-size == n$}
			\State{$\text{\textbf{error} "Heap overflow"}$}
			\EndIf{} 

			\State{$A.heap-size = A.heap-size + 1$}
			\State{$k = x.key$}
			\State{$x.key = –\infty$}
			\State{$A[A.heap-size] = x$}
			\State{$map x to index heap-size in the array$}

			\EndProcedure{}
		\end{algorithmic}
	\end{minipage}
\end{center}
